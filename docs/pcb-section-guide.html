Best board size

Selected: 100 x 80 mm, 2 layers, 1.6 mm FR-4, 1 oz copper. The footprints add up to about 3896 mm² of the 8000 mm² board (49 %), but the real constraint is the shape: the HLK module is 38 x 23 mm and the 1x20 display header is 51 mm long, the two relays need 19 mm each, and the left column needs a 6.4 mm slot. A 90 x 70 mm board would not hold the relays, HLK and header with the 6.4 mm slot rule; 100 x 80 mm does, with room for routing and for a small enclosure rail.

Edge plan

  • Top edge: J2 and J3 (mains, left), J8 / J7 (OLED / 16x2), J12 spare, U.FL end of the ESP32 module.
  • Left edge: J4 and J5 relay terminals (COM / NO / NC).
  • Bottom edge: J6 (PZEM), J10 / J11 (float inputs), J1 (USB-C).
  • Right edge: J9 (12864 LCD, 1x20).

Not on the board: ANT1 (U.FL to SMA pigtail) and ANT2 (SMA antenna). The ESP32 module has no PCB antenna; the pigtail goes to a bulkhead outside the enclosure.

Mains input 230 V0 / 3 placed

Hazardous side. No fuse or varistor on board (owner decision): protect upstream.

  • Mains parts stay left of the red slot. J2 and J3 sit on the top edge so the cables enter from the top.
  • U2 (HLK-5M05, 38 x 23 mm) lies across the slot: AC pins on the left end, 5 V pins on the right end. Check the real pin pitch on the HLK drawing before routing the slot.
  • Keep 6.4 mm (8 mm better) between any mains copper and low-voltage copper, 2 mm from mains copper to the board edge. No pour or vias across the slot.

Order of work

  1. In EasyEDA PCB, set the board outline to 100 x 80 mm with 3 mm corners and place the four M3 pads (4, 4), (96, 4), (4, 76), (96, 76) mm.
  2. Draw the isolation slot (x 28.1 to 29.4 mm, y 7 to 69 mm) on the board-outline layer as a routed slot. Add keep-out rectangles 3.2 mm around it on both copper layers.
  3. Import the schematic (Design > Update/Convert Schematic to PCB). Check every footprint against its datasheet first: J1 USB-C, K1/K2 relays, U2 HLK, KF128 terminals, J9 1x20.
  4. Place section by section in this order: mains, relays, power, USB, ESP32, displays, PZEM and I/O. Use the X / Y tables as a start, then tick each part here.
  5. Draw the net classes from the table above (widths and the USB pair), then pour GND on the low-voltage side only.
  6. Route mains and relay contacts first, then 5 V and 3.3 V, then USB, then the rest by hand.
  7. Run the PCB DRC with the 6.4 mm mains clearance rule applied between the mains and low-voltage net classes. Add silkscreen: MAINS WARNING, COM / NO / NC labels, J2 L and N.
  8. Export Gerber and have the PCB maker check the slot and 6.4 mm spacing. Do not power from mains before the engineer review (PLAN.md section 14).
