Best board size

Selected: 100 x 90 mm, 2 layers, 1.6 mm FR-4, 1 oz copper. The footprints add up to about 4016 mm² of the 9000 mm² board (45 %), but the real constraint is the shape: the HLK module is 38 x 23 mm, the 1x20 display header is 51 mm long, the two relays need 19 mm each, and the left column needs a 6.4 mm slot. 100 x 100 mm or smaller was the limit set by the owner: 100 x 90 mm stays inside it and in the cheapest PCB price tier. A 100 x 80 mm board no longer fits once the J13 UART header, the 6.4 mm clearance to the AC terminals and the 51 mm display header are all kept.

Edge plan

  • Top edge: J2 and J3 (mains, left), J8 (OLED) and J7 (16x2), J13 (UART / boot header), U.FL end of the ESP32 module.
  • Left edge: J4 and J5 relay terminals (COM / NO / NC).
  • Bottom edge: J6 (PZEM), J10 and J11 (float inputs), J1 (USB-C).
  • Right edge: J9 (12864 LCD, 1x20) and J12 (spare).

Not on the board: ANT1 (U.FL to SMA pigtail) and ANT2 (SMA antenna). The ESP32 module has no PCB antenna; the pigtail goes to a bulkhead outside the enclosure.

Silkscreen box per block: a good idea

  • Why it helps: you see at a glance which parts belong to relay 1, relay 2, power or the ESP32, which makes assembly, probing and fault finding much easier.
  • The mains boxes are a safety aid: draw MAINS IN and POWER with a thicker line and a MAINS 230 V title so nobody probes them by accident.
  • Rules: use the Top Silkscreen layer; keep box lines 1 mm from pads (the fab removes silkscreen on pads anyway); never draw a box line across the isolation slot or a mounting hole; keep titles at least 0.8 mm high.
  • This plan: 13 dashed boxes, one per block. The checker confirms no box overlaps another and no foreign part sits inside a box.
  • In EasyEDA: place each block as a group, then draw its rectangle and title on the Top Silkscreen layer.

Order of work

  1. In EasyEDA PCB the board outline (100 x 90 mm, 3 mm corners), the isolation slot and the four 3.2 mm mounting holes (4, 4), (96, 4), (4, 86), (96, 86) mm already exist.
  2. Import the schematic (Design > Update/Convert Schematic to PCB). Check every footprint against its datasheet first: J1 USB-C, K1/K2 relays, U2 HLK, KF128 terminals, J9 1x20.
  3. Place block by block with the X / Y tables: mains in, power, relay 1, relay 2, ESP32, displays, USB, PZEM, inputs, buttons. Tick each part here.
  4. Draw one rectangle per block on the Top Silkscreen layer with the block title (dashed boxes in the floor plan). Keep the box edges 1 mm off the pads; the silkscreen is removed from pads anyway.
  5. Draw the net classes from the table above (widths and the USB pair), then pour GND on the low-voltage side only.
  6. Route mains and relay contacts first, then 5 V and 3.3 V, then USB, then the rest by hand.
  7. Run the PCB DRC with a 6.4 mm clearance rule between the mains and low-voltage net classes. Add silkscreen: MAINS WARNING, COM / NO / NC labels, J2 L and N, pin 1 marks.
  8. Export Gerber and have the PCB maker check the slot and the 6.4 mm spacing. No mains power before the engineer review (PLAN.md section 14).
